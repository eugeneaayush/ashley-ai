# Context for the zf-ashley-moat research run

## Who is asking

The founder/operator of Ashley.Ai (legal entity Helen Labs, Inc.; site https://www.tryashley.ai).
Their words: "My company needs a strong competitive moat, because competitors could very easily
replicate the idea. We have trained AI models in-house to create very realistic avatars that can
interview candidates. Brainstorm some ways we can build a competitive moat."

## What the product is (from the public site, fetched 2026-09-23; full text in site-home.txt and site-subpages.txt)

- An AI interviewer for high-volume hiring (retail, support, sales, ops). Every applicant gets a
  live, adaptive video interview with a photoreal avatar; scored on the employer's rubric from the
  transcript only; a human makes every decision; 11 languages; live captions.
- The avatar is rendered frame by frame in real time by a rendering/inference stack the company
  built and operates itself, not a rented avatar API. The site calls this "the slowest thing we
  built and the hardest to copy" and says it is why competitors ship "a phone call or a chat box".
- Trust posture as a feature: Ashley discloses she is an AI at the start; appearance and accent are
  never scored; no auto-rejection; "you won't be ghosted"; candidate FAQ page; GDPR/CCPA handling;
  positioned to support NYC Local Law 144 and Illinois AI-hiring laws (AIVIA, HB 3773) audits.
- Pricing: pay-as-you-go $6 per completed interview; Growth $499/mo for 100 interviews ($5.50
  overage); Scale $1,499/mo for 400 ($5.00 overage); Enterprise (custom avatar, compliance pack,
  SSO, DPA, SLA, native ATS integration). Month to month. "Early-access pricing".
- The site publishes comparison pages: Ashley vs Alex ("the enterprise voice interviewer,
  formerly Apriora"), vs Ribbon ("the self-serve voice interviewer for hourly hiring"),
  vs HireVue ("the enterprise incumbent"). Find them via https://www.tryashley.ai/sitemap.xml.
- Stated users: teams at the U.S. Air Force Academy and the University of Florida's Warrington
  College of Business. Partner logos: NVIDIA Inception, Cloudflare for Startups, RunPod, UChicago,
  UPenn. Developer surface is "guided, not self-serve": no public API, webhooks rolling out,
  no public MCP server.
- Vision page: the long-term asset is "a structured, consistent record of how people answer
  questions about the work, at a scale human interviewing has never reached", as the foundation
  for knowing "what a strong candidate for a given role actually sounds like".

## Unknowns the director will ask the founder (do not guess; state assumptions)

Traction (customers, interviews to date), team size, runway, and which segment they are winning in.
Assume an early-stage startup (a small team, months not years of runway) unless evidence says otherwise.

## What a useful answer looks like

A "moat" here means something a well-funded competitor, including one renting a third-party
avatar API, could not replicate within roughly 12 months, or would not want to. Prefer moats an
early-stage company can start building this quarter. Every claim needs a source_url, a verbatim
quote of at most 40 words where one exists, and the date fetched. Never invent products,
customers, prices, funding rounds, laws, or dates.
